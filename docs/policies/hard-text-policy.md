# Hard-Text Policy (DRAFT — Simon to edit)

Applies to: Akedah, the Flood, the plagues / death of the firstborn, Egyptians in the sea,
Korach, Nadav & Avihu, Pinchas, Amalek, Haman's fate, Tazria-Metzora, wars in Bamidbar/Devarim,
deaths of Sarah, Rachel, Aharon, Moshe. Framing: Modern Orthodox (D3).

## On the card (front + back)
1. **Feeling + choice, not consequence.** Tell the story through what a character felt and chose.
   ("Pharaoh kept saying NO. How did the people feel?") Never describe death, injury or punishment.
2. **Stop the story at a safe beat.** E.g. the Akedah card ends at the angel calling "Avraham!"
   and the ram; the sea card ends with the Jewish people singing on the other side.
3. **Hashem is never scary on a card.** Hashem protects, helps and loves; images = light, clouds, a hand.
4. **Villains are "making bad choices," not monsters.** No snarling, no pointing, no weapons.
5. **Midrash is labeled** in a Modern Orthodox voice: "Our Sages teach us…"

## In the teacher guide booklet (never on the card)
6. **A scripted answer for the question kids will ask** ("What happened to Haman?",
   "Did the Egyptians die?"). 1–2 honest, calm sentences + redirect.
   Example: "Haman's bad plan didn't work, and he wasn't able to hurt anyone anymore.
   What do you think the Jewish people did to celebrate?"
7. **The pshat in brief**, with verse refs, so the teacher knows what the text actually says.
8. **When to skip.** Parshiyot that are mostly law (e.g. Tazria-Metzora) choose a single positive
   theme (e.g. "speaking kindly" — shmirat halashon) rather than depicting the subject.

## Process
- The Sensitivity Reviewer (02b) flags every card touching this list after the Curriculum Designer step.
- Simon approves the framing at that checkpoint, before content is written.
